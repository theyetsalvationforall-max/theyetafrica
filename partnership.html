How a partnership conversation works

We aim to build relationships carefully, with shared understanding and realistic commitments.

01

Introduce your organisation

Tell us who you are, where you work and what matters most to your ministry or organisation.

02

Explore shared priorities

We can discuss the areas of work that align, the people and communities involved, and possible next steps.

03

Agree a practical way forward

Where there is a good fit, clarify responsibilities, timelines, communication and how activities will be reviewed.

Our commitment: We want partnership conversations to be transparent and responsible. Specific programmes, budgets, outcomes and reporting arrangements should be discussed and agreed before any commitment is made.
Trust · Stewardship · Clarity

Accountability and responsible partnership

We believe meaningful collaboration should be grounded in honest communication, agreed responsibilities and careful stewardship.

Clear purpose and written scope

Before a project begins, partners should agree on its purpose, intended activities, roles, timeline and any expected costs in writing.

Responsible financial stewardship

Where financial support is discussed, the parties should agree on an approved budget, authorised handling arrangements, supporting records and a reporting schedule before funds are transferred.

Updates and reporting

Project updates should describe activities completed, progress against agreed plans, challenges and how resources were used. Supporting documentation should be retained where appropriate.

Safeguarding and dignity

Activities involving children, young people or vulnerable people should be planned with appropriate safeguarding, consent, privacy and referral arrangements.

Conflicts of interest

Potential conflicts should be disclosed early and handled openly so that decisions are made in the best interests of the ministry and the communities served.

Mutual review and learning

Partners should agree how to review outcomes, learn from experience, raise concerns and make changes when circumstances require it.

Important: This framework describes the standards proposed for partnership arrangements; it is not a claim that the ministry has independent audits, formal certifications or policies that have not been published. Partners should request and review relevant organisational, financial and safeguarding documents before entering a funded or formal agreement.

Common questions

Partnership questions

Who can explore a partnership?

Churches, Christian ministries, faith-based organisations, foundations, businesses and individuals are welcome to introduce themselves.

Can organisations outside Uganda partner with you?

Yes. We welcome enquiries from organisations and individuals in other African countries and internationally.

Does an enquiry confirm a partnership?

No. An enquiry begins a conversation. Any collaboration will depend on mutual agreement, capacity and clear expectations.

Can I offer prayer or practical resources?

Yes. You can select prayer or resource partnership in the enquiry form and describe what you have in mind.
